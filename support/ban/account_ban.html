Account Ban Information

What to do if your account
has been banned

This page explains what a ban means, why it may have happened, what access is affected, and how you can request a review if you believe the action was made in error.

What this means

A ban means access to your account or device has been blocked by our security and moderation systems. This is a stronger action than a warning or temporary restriction and is usually applied when activity appears to seriously break our rules or put the platform, its content, or other users at risk.

Depending on the type of ban, you may be unable to sign in, verify devices, use premium features, access protected materials, or interact with parts of the platform that require an active account.

Common reasons for a ban

Automated scraping or bulk downloading

Using bots, scripts, or automated tools to collect protected resources, predicted papers, or premium materials.

Sharing restricted content

Re-uploading, redistributing, or posting protected RevisionBase content on other websites, drives, chats, or social platforms.

Bypassing security systems

Attempting to avoid device checks, abuse account linking, share access, or interfere with login and verification systems.

Abusive or harmful behaviour

Harassment, threats, spam, impersonation, or other conduct that harms users or disrupts the platform.

Repeated policy breaches

Multiple warnings, repeated misuse, or continued activity after a previous restriction may lead to a full ban.

What may be affected

Login access Blocked

Support appeal access Available

Some bans are temporary, while others are permanent. The exact outcome depends on the seriousness of the incident, any previous flags, and the result of any appeal review.

What to do next

1

Read the reason shown on the login popup

When available, your ban notice will include a short reason, such as suspicious automation, misuse of protected materials, or a manual moderation decision.

2

Review the platform rules

Make sure you understand which behaviour may have triggered the ban, especially around account sharing, scraping, bypass attempts, or content redistribution.

3

Submit an appeal if you believe this was a mistake

Use the appeal form to explain what happened clearly and honestly. Include any useful context, such as shared devices, school networks, browser issues, or anything else that may help a moderator review the case properly.

4

Wait for the review decision

Once submitted, appeals are reviewed in order. Repeated submissions or abuse of the appeal process may delay review.

Review process

How appeals are reviewed

Appeals are reviewed using the information stored by our security systems and moderation tools. This may include login attempts, device verification records, unusual usage patterns, and other account safety signals. A successful appeal may result in access being restored, while serious or repeated cases may remain banned.

Case reviewed

Moderation and system logs are checked.

Decision sent

You may receive an outcome after review.

Final action

Access is restored, limited, or kept blocked.

Frequently asked questions

Is a ban always permanent?

No. Some bans are temporary and expire after review or after a set period. Others may be permanent, especially where there is serious misuse, repeat offences, or confirmed attempts to bypass platform security.

Can I make a new account instead?

No. Creating new accounts or using new devices to avoid a ban may lead to further enforcement and may reduce the chance of a successful appeal.

How long does an appeal take?

Most appeals are reviewed within a few days, but this may take longer during busy periods or where extra checks are needed.

What should I include in my appeal?

Include a clear explanation of what happened, whether the activity was intentional, whether the device is shared, and any details that might explain unusual behaviour. Honest, specific appeals are more useful than very short or vague ones.

Can I still access any part of RevisionBase?

That depends on the type of ban. Some users may still be able to view limited public pages, while protected areas, account features, and verification systems remain blocked.
