Decode the Mark Scheme

A-Levels aren't just about what you know—they are about how you use it. Understanding AO1, AO2, and AO3 is the secret to moving from a B to an A*.

1
K

AO1: Knowledge

"Demonstrate knowledge and understanding of scientific ideas, processes, techniques and procedures."

  • Defining terms accurately
  • Citing theories/studies
  • Using correct terminology
2
A

AO2: Application

"Apply knowledge and understanding to theoretical and practical contexts."

  • Linking theory to the 'Item'
  • Solving novel problems
  • Contextualising your answer
3
E

AO3: Evaluation

"Analyse, interpret and evaluate scientific information, ideas and evidence."

  • Strengths & Weaknesses
  • Competing arguments
  • Justified conclusions

Subject-Specific Breakdowns

Select a subject to see how marks are distributed.

Psychology (AQA)

In a 16-mark essay, the split is usually 6 marks for Description (AO1) and 10 marks for Evaluation (AO3).

AO1 Tip

Don't over-describe. You only have 6 marks. Keep outlining concise.

AO3 Tip

Use the "PEEL" structure: Point, Evidence, Explain, Link back to the question.

History

Heavily weighted towards Argument (AO3) and Knowledge (AO1). Source questions introduce AO2 (Analysis of provenance).

The "So What?" Test

For every fact (AO1) you write, you must explain its significance (AO3) to the question.

English Literature

AO1 (Articulate argument), AO2 (Analysis of methods), AO3 (Context), AO4 (Connections), AO5 (Alternative interpretations).

Context is King (AO3)

Don't bolt on history. Context must illuminate the text.

Biology / Chemistry / Physics

AO1 (Recall), AO2 (Apply to unknown situations), AO3 (Analyze data/Practical skills).

The AO2 Trap

Most students lose marks here by just reciting notes instead of using the data provided in the question.
